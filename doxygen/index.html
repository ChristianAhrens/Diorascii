Diorascii

Animated ASCII scenes for your terminal: an underwater aquarium, a forest with a river, a desert with an oasis, a mountain range from the meadow up to the snow, a city with skyscrapers, traffic and trains, the surface of the moon, and more to come. Written in C++20 as one small, self-contained binary with no runtime dependencies. Truecolor with automatic 256-color fallback, or a terminal-themed look that follows your own colors.

Download

Every release on the releases page contains an installer per platform, which puts diorascii into your PATH so that you can run it from any terminal. Nothing else needs to be installed.

Platform Asset Notes
macOS Diorascii-x.y.z-macOS.pkg universal (Apple silicon and Intel), installer to /usr/local/bin
Windows Diorascii-x.y.z-Windows-Setup.exe installer to Program Files, adds the PATH, Windows Terminal or Windows 10+ console
Linux (Debian, Ubuntu) Diorascii-x.y.z-Linux.deb sudo apt install ./Diorascii-x.y.z-Linux.deb
Linux (Fedora, openSUSE) Diorascii-x.y.z-Linux.rpm sudo dnf install ./Diorascii-x.y.z-Linux.rpm
Linux, other Diorascii-x.y.z-Linux.tar.gz 64-bit, no extra libraries; unpack and run bin/diorascii
Windows, portable Diorascii-x.y.z-Windows.zip unpack and run bin\diorascii.exe

Installing a newer version over an older one upgrades it in place. To uninstall: on Windows use Apps > Installed apps (or the uninstaller in the install folder), on Debian/Fedora the package manager (sudo apt remove diorascii, sudo dnf remove diorascii), and on macOS, which has no uninstaller for packages: 

sudo rm /usr/local/bin/diorascii && sudo rm -r /usr/local/share/doc/diorascii
sudo pkgutil --forget com.ChristianAhrens.Diorascii

The Windows installer is not code-signed, so SmartScreen may ask for confirmation ("More info", "Run anyway"). The macOS installer attached to a release is signed and notarized by the owner; the one the CI builds (only on the workflow's draft, before it is replaced) is ad hoc signed, and macOS asks for confirmation (right-click, Open).

Usage

diorascii                       # m menu, left/right scene, f action, x rare event (X: the second one), c style, t time of day, i info, p pause, r restart, q quit
diorascii --scene moon          # start in a specific scene (--list-scenes shows all)
diorascii --mute                # start without the ambient sound; a toggles it (see Sound below)
diorascii --console             # force the Linux text console look (picked automatically on TERM=linux, also inside tmux)
diorascii --style terminal      # 16 ANSI colors on your own background (also: mono, full)
diorascii --dump 100            # headless: print the scene after 100 steps
diorascii --dump 200 --act 20   # ...and trigger the scene's action at step 20
diorascii --dump 400 --rare 20  # ...or summon the scene's rare event at step 20
diorascii --dump 400 --rare-other 20  # ...or its second, less likely catastrophe
diorascii --hour 22             # start at 22:00 (scenes with a day and night cycle)
diorascii --realtime            # let the time of day follow this computer's clock
diorascii --gallery             # print every sprite (art review)
diorascii --assets ./my-art     # add or override sprites and scenes from a directory

The menu (m, Enter or Esc) lists scene, style, time of day, info line, the scene's action, the rare event, the other rare event, resume and quit, and shows the version and the copyright at the bottom. It is drawn into the same cell grid as the scene and driven only by abstract actions (up, down, left, right, select, back), so any input device can use it.

Scenes and the action key

f (or F) triggers the scene's primary action. What it does depends on the scene:

Scene Action What happens
Aquarium Feed the fish a clump of eight twinkling flakes of food sinks from the surface (at most 30 at once); fish swim over and eat them, and the action's name flashes on screen so you can tell it worked
Forest Make it rain it rains for about 18 seconds (pressing again keeps it going): drops streak down at a slant and splash on the bank and in the river, clouds roll in and the light dims, then it clears up again
Desert Sandstorm or tornado a coin toss each time. A sandstorm blows grains across the whole screen for about 16 seconds while the sky turns ochre and the sun disappears. A tornado crosses the scene instead, weaving, whirling up sand and throwing every animal in its path into the air; they land dazed, and then walk on. Pressing again during a tornado brings the sandstorm on top of it.
Mountains Snowstorm an icy wind sweeps snow across the whole screen for about 22 seconds: flakes race sideways while they settle, the peaks and the sun vanish in a pale whiteout, the birds fall silent and the wind howls (see below)
City Call an ambulance an ambulance drives through the streets with its lights flashing
Moon Meteor strike a meteorite streaks down and hits the ground: it wrecks everything standing there, digs a crater and throws up debris and dust that drift back down slowly under the moon's low gravity. Dust then fills the crater gradually, shallow rim first, until the ground is smooth again. Rovers drive down into the crater and climb out as it fills.

New scenes define their own action in their *.scene file (see below).

The city

Buildings in three rows (a pale far row, a middle row and a front row) stand behind a narrow main street: a sidewalk, a road of two lanes and a sidewalk. They run from low offices to towers of up to 80% of the sky, with four kinds of facade (grid of windows, glass curtain wall, ribbons, small windows) and roofs with antennas, crowns, water towers, helipads or steps, and their windows light up as it gets dark. Street lamps switch on at dusk and pool light on the street. Cars, taxis, buses, trucks and cyclists keep to their side of the road, and the traffic follows the rush hours: it is thin at night and at midday and peaks at about 8:00 and 17:30 (a vehicle sprite with rush: yes only joins the street with the chance that the time of day gives), people walk on both sidewalks (fewer at night), and there are shops, a kiosk and a park with a fountain, benches and trees. An elevated railway runs through the buildings on a viaduct (a dark deck with bright rails on a concrete beam with pillars, masts and a wire above it) with a station, and trains run on it now and then (on screens too small for it there is no railway). On wide enough screens one to three side streets break off from the main street at a broad crossing with a zebra stripe, run a short way back between the buildings and disappear behind them: some cars turn off the main road into them, showing three-quarter and rear views as they turn and drive away, and others come down them, seen from the front, and turn onto the main road. Between the buildings of the front row (which are spaced out) you can sometimes see other side streets running back behind them. Planes come in on their landing approach in the background, sinking slowly behind the skyline towards an airport that is off screen. Vehicles, trains and planes are drawn as fine line art on a darkened backing so that they stand out. The buildings are generated to the height of your terminal, always lower than the mountains' peaks.

The mountains

Unlike the other scenes, which are mostly ground or mostly sky or mostly sea, the mountains use the whole height of the terminal. From the bottom up: a green meadow with a small stream, a belt of forest and bushes, bare rock with crags and dwarf pines, and snow and ice on the peaks, with the sky above them. The peaks (a different range every run) are tall and the zones are bands of altitude whose edges wobble from column to column, so the tree line and the snow line are never straight. The far flank of every peak lies in shadow, the rock is streaked with gullies, and the sun and the moon set behind the ridges.

Everything lives in its own zone and stays there:

Who Where What they do
Eagles the sky, down as far as the rock soar across the screen high up and now and then swoop down, wings folded, towards the rocky parts
Goats the rock and the snow leap along the ledges in hops, climb to other ledges and turn round where the rock ends instead of walking into the sky
Deer the meadow and the forest stroll about; they are the prey
Bears the meadow and the forest stroll about, then run a deer down and feast on it (one kill each, as with the lion)
Small birds over the forest and the meadow flit about by day
Trees, crags, spires forest, rock, snow firs and bushes in the forest, dwarf pines and crags in the rock, ice spires in the snow

Things lower on the screen are nearer and drawn in front. Like everywhere, most animals sleep at night.

Avalanche (the rare event, x summons it): high on a snowy flank the snow starts to creak and trickle, then the slab lets go and a powder cloud rips down the mountainside, cutting a swath through the rock and the forest down into the valley and across the stream. Trees, bushes and animals in its path are buried; birds and eagles are above it. The white swath, scattered with rubble, lies over the land and fades after about 40 seconds, the water coming back last; what was buried grows or walks back in over the next minute.

Day and night

The aquarium, the forest, the desert and the mountains have a day and night cycle (a full day takes four minutes). The sun crosses the sky by day and the moon by night, the sky and the water darken and turn blue at night with a red glow at sunrise and sunset, and stars come out. Creatures can belong to the day or the night: butterflies and birds by day, fireflies by night. Clouds from the forest's rain dim the day, too. The moon has a cycle of its own: it has no atmosphere, so the sky stays black with its stars all the time. By day a large sun stands high in the sky over a slightly lighter grey ground; by night the earth rises over a darker, cooler one.

At night most animals sleep: a share of every species (about 70% for most) disappears and stays frozen where it is, each at its own bedtime, so herds and schools thin out one after another over dusk and wake up one after another at dawn. Sleepers do not eat, hunt or get hunted. A few animals stay up, and some are nocturnal, like the desert's bats and the lion.

t (or the menu) steps through cycle, dawn, day, dusk, night, real time: the first runs the clock, the next four freeze the time of day, and real time follows the clock of the computer Diorascii runs on (also --realtime), so the scene's day, dusk and night match the one outside. --hour sets the start time. The time of day carries over when you switch scenes. The info line (i) shows the clock.

In the terminal styles the cycle shows in the stars, the sun and moon and the creatures coming and going; the colors themselves stay with your terminal theme.

Sound

Ambient sound is synthesized at run time (no recordings) and on by default: --mute starts without it, and the a key or the menu's Sound entry switch it. Without a sound device the app simply stays silent. Every scene has its own soundscape, and the sound follows what happens on screen:

Scene What you hear
Forest soft wind in the trees that swells with slow gusts, deeper in a storm; crickets at night; rain. A thundercloud on its way brings distant rumbling and far-off claps; a lightning strike cracks at the moment of the screen flash, then the thunder rolls in. In a flood there is the deep, rushing roar of the water
Aquarium a deep underwater hum, bubbles rising now and then, a small blip for every flake of food a fish eats, the song of a passing whale the growl of the kraken as it nears, and a muffled double gulp for every animal its tentacles swallow. In the poisoned sea there is the hiss of gas and the glugging of bubbles
Desert a dry, steady wind, a few crickets at night; in a sandstorm the wind howls with the hiss of blown sand, and a tornado crossing the scene adds a deep, turning roar. The sandworm shakes the ground as it nears, bellows with a very deep, trumpeting roar while it crosses, and each animal it swallows is chewed with a series of low, grumbling crunches. The poachers' helicopter beats the air with its rotor, and every rifle shot cracks
Mountains thin alpine wind, a gurgling stream and now and then, by day, the shrill cry of an eagle or a woodpecker drumming on a tree. A snowstorm brings a howling icy wind with a gliding whistle and the hiss of ice crystals, and silences the eagle and the woodpecker; an avalanche creaks, then breaks with a burst of crackling and swells into a deep rumble with the roar of tumbling snow and rock, crackling and thumping as it knocks down trees and boulders on its way, and every animal it buries lets out a short, muffled squeal. The yeti's steps thud through the ground and it roars now and then
City a steady murmur of traffic that thins out at night, cars passing by with a sinking engine note, a horn (sometimes two honks, sometimes one long blast) from time to time, the quick two-tone siren of the ambulance and the slow, wailing siren of the fire engine (which drives off without it once the fire is out), trains that rumble and beat out the regular "klack-klack, klack-klack" of the wheels over the rail joints and sound their horn as they arrive, a faint jet when a plane is on its approach, and the roar and crackle of a burning skyscraper. When the monsters fight, their heavy steps shake the ground and the lizard's deep bellow alternates with the winged one's shrill screech (which is gone for good once the winged one has fallen); every stream of fire has its own roaring, crackling whoosh
Moon no air, so only what could be felt through the ground: a faint, deep drone. A meteor strike thuds up through the ground, with a dull rumble and debris pattering down slowly; the alien's flash comes with an eerie warble. The big meteor is a rumble in the ground that swells as it comes down, ending in the thud of the impact

Rare events

Every scene has a rare catastrophe that turns up every few minutes (the first one after two to five minutes) and wipes out life. It is announced by its arrival: it crosses the scene, strikes halfway across, and moves on. Press x, or choose Rare event in the menu, to summon it right away instead of waiting.

Every scene has a second catastrophe (see below), which replaces the first one one time in five (the weights in the scene's --- rare list are 1 and 0.25). Press X (shift), or choose Other rare event in the menu (--rare-other for --dump), to see it right away; a plain x picks by the odds like the timer does.

Scene Catastrophe What happens
Aquarium Kraken a giant sea monster lumbers across the sea bed, the water darkens to violet, and it lashes out with tentacles at the animals around it as it goes: whatever a tentacle reaches (fish, jellyfish, eels, crabs, shark, whale) bursts into ink, and animals out of reach survive
Aquarium Leaking submarine second catastrophe: a grey submarine (a solid hull with a dashed and slashed outline) drives through the sea at mid depth and leaves a trail of poison behind it; every puff of the trail spreads while the submarine passes on, until the cloud fills the sea from top to bottom, and every animal it reaches dies. The water turns sickly green along the trail; after the submarine has left, the cloud lingers and thins out, and the sea slowly fills with life again
Forest Thunderstorm a dark cloud rolls across the sky and it starts to pour; a lightning bolt hits the ground under the cloud, the screen flashes twice, and every animal dies
Forest Flood second catastrophe: the river bursts its banks in a downpour and a wall of water runs across the land from one side. Its front is a sparse, tumbling wave of foam that fills in to the solid, muddy flood within a few steps behind it, and everything it reaches (every tree, bush, flower and animal on the bank, logs ride on its front) is ripped off. The water stands over the land for a while and recedes, and leaves mud and bare, devastated ground that is only replanted slowly over the next minute or so
Desert Sandworm a huge banded worm with a toothed maw crawls through the dunes in a cloud of dust and lashes out a tongue at the animals on the ground ahead of it and swallows each one it reaches (what flies is spared)
Desert Poachers second catastrophe: a helicopter with poachers on board flies after the closest animal, hovers over it and shoots down everything alive (what flies as well) one animal after the other, with tracer lines and rifle cracks; it leaves when nothing is left, and the animals come back later
Mountains Avalanche the snow on a high flank cracks, then a powder cloud rips down the mountain, through the forest and into the stream, burying trees, bushes and animals in its swath
Mountains Yeti second catastrophe: an abnormally large, white-furred yeti climbs over the highest ridge from the back (it first rises out of the mountain's outline, hidden by it), then comes down the slope into the valley and hunts down the animals on the ground: it does not strike from a distance but has to walk right up to each animal, so it crosses the scene again and again; it roars and its steps thud; then it goes back up and over (what flies is out of its reach)
City Fire a skyscraper catches fire: flames lick up its facade and over the roof, smoke billows, a fire engine races through the street with its siren, parks in front of the building and sprays it with jets of blue water, which puts the fire out sooner, and drives off without its siren as soon as the last fire is out. Most of the time the fire is beaten and the building is wrapped in scaffolding with a crane and renovated with new looks. Sometimes the fire wins: it spreads to 3 to 5 neighbouring buildings one after another, they all burn down (they collapse into smouldering rubble), the engine leaves when the flames are out, and it takes a long while, about a minute and a half, before everything is rebuilt from the rubble. From the arrival of the engine until the last building is back, the site (wider if the fire has spread) is cordoned off with barriers across the road and both sidewalks: all other vehicles and people stop in front of them and wait
City Monster fight second catastrophe: a giant lizard with glowing blue spines walks in from one side and a winged, spiny monster flies in from the other and lands; both are drawn as dark, solid bodies with a clear outline of dashes and slashes. They fight by spitting streams of fire at each other, blue from the lizard and violet from the winged one (now one, now the other, now both at once), each about 33 rows tall. Everyone on the streets, people and vehicles alike, panics and runs off the screen as soon as the monsters appear, and nothing new comes until it is over. As soon as they start to fight, fire spreads from where they stand outwards: building after building catches fire, burns for a few seconds and comes down, until the winged one falls and the lizard walks on across the city. Dozens of buildings are left as ruins; once the fight is over, the rebuilding starts in the middle and spreads outwards in the same way
Moon Alien an alien walks across the surface and destroys the flagpole and the rover each at the moment it passes them (a rover that drives out of its way is spared), and nothing else
Moon Big meteor second catastrophe: a speck appears in the black sky, far away, and grows bigger and bigger as it falls (a rumble builds up in the ground); it hits the surface and the impact spans the entire width of the screen: the whole screen flashes, a crater is dug across the full width (deepest where it hit), everything standing on the ground is wrecked, and a wave of dust and thrown rock runs out to both edges and drifts back down slowly under the low gravity. Rovers, flags, rocks and the lander are replaced later

The avalanche is the exception to "crosses the scene": it comes down a mountainside (see above) and buries what is in its path instead of everything alive.

The dead do not stay dead: they come back one by one over the next minute or so, animals walking in from the edges and the flagpole planted again. Sleeping animals die unseen.

Build

scripts/build.sh            # macOS / Linux  -> build/<preset>/src/diorascii
scripts/build.ps1           # Windows        -> build\release\src\Release\diorascii.exe

Needs only CMake 3.21 or newer and a C++20 compiler. scripts/build.sh dev makes a debug build and runs the tests. Assets are embedded into the binary at build time. -DDIORASCII_VERSION=x.y.z sets the version the binary reports. tvOS (macOS with Xcode only): the Apple TV app is generated and built by CMake, there is no hand-made Xcode project. 

cmake -S . -B build/tvos -G Xcode -DCMAKE_SYSTEM_NAME=tvOS -DCMAKE_OSX_SYSROOT=appletvsimulator
cmake --build build/tvos --target Diorascii -- CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO
xcrun simctl install booted build/tvos/apple/tvOS/Debug-appletvsimulator/Diorascii.app

The generated project serves both the simulator and devices (pick the destination in Xcode); configure with -DCMAKE_OSX_SYSROOT=appletvos -DDIORASCII_APPLE_TEAM=<team id> to sign for a device. Siri Remote: swipe left/right changes the scene, swipe up the time of day, swipe down the sound, click runs the scene's action, Play/Pause pauses, holding the click opens the menu (swipes and click then navigate it), Menu closes the menu or leaves the app.

With GCC the C++ runtime is linked statically for a single-file binary when the static library is installed (Fedora: dnf install libstdc++-static); without it the build falls back to the shared one.

Extending: sprites

A *.sprite file is a header of key: value lines followed by one or more --- art frames, each optionally followed by a --- mask. Drop it into a directory and pass --assets DIR (or put it in assets/ to ship it). 

name: darter
facing: right            # direction the art points
habitat: medium          # medium (water) | surface | sky | ground | river | rail (the city's elevated railway)
speed: 9..18             # cells per second
depth: 3..16             # draw order, larger = further away
tag: prey                # lets behaviors find it (prey, food, ...)
behaviors: seek_food swim emit_bubbles
pool: warm               # color pool for mask digits 1-9: warm | cool | earth
--- art
<=={o>
--- mask
2111W1

Header keys:

Key Meaning
name, facing, habitat, speed, depth, tag, behaviors, pool as in the example
fps, loop: no animation speed; stop on the last frame
die-after-animation: yes remove the sprite when its animation has played once
color: #rrggbb default color (used for blank mask cells)
random-frame: yes start on a random frame (for particles)
ttl: 2..4 lifetime in seconds
slope diagonal flight: vertical speed relative to horizontal
anchor-row which sprite row sits on the surface line
mouth-row row where it eats or bites
bubble-rate, bob-amplitude, bob-frequency tuning for those behaviors
behaviors: drive, turns: 0.3 a city vehicle that drives along the main road and, with this chance, turns off into the next side street
feet-rows: 1..-2, traffic: yes a ground sprite's feet stand on these ground rows (0 is the first, a negative number counts from the bottom); with traffic vehicles keep to their side of the road: the lower rows of the range moving right, the upper rows moving left
lanes: 4 ground sprites stand up to this many rows deep on the bank; the ones further forward are drawn in front
altitude: 8 sky sprites hover within this many rows above the ground
active: day or night only shown by day or by night (needs a day and night cycle in the scene)
glow: yes not dimmed at night (fireflies, sun, moon)
sleeps: 0.7 this share of the animals sleeps at night (frozen and hidden, each from its own sun height); needs a day and night cycle
herd: 4..7, herd-spacing spawned in herds of this size that move together; members that leave come back from the same side facing the same way. In a scene's population one unit means one herd
contrast: yes dark strokes where the background behind a cell is light (pale sand), the sprite's own color where it is dark (full style only)
feast-time, hunt-effect for hunters: how long they stay put after a kill, and the sprite shown at the kill
dust-rate puffs of dust kicked up per second by emit_dust
wind: yes a particle that is blown across the whole scene (sand), for a weather action
life: yes a living thing: catastrophes that wipe out life take it
row: 2 pinned to this row (a negative number counts from the bottom)
wipes, strike-mode, strike-at, strike-reach, strike-aim, strike-lashes, lash-color, strike-effect for catastrophes (behavior catastrophe): life or a list of sprite names to wipe out; how it strikes (centre, the default: everything at once when it is strike-at (0 to 1) of the way across the scene, like a storm; sweep: each victim as the middle of the attacker passes it; devour: each victim in its way as its front reaches it, and with strike-reach: N it lashes a tongue or tentacle out at victims up to N cells away, drawn from the row mouth-row in lash-color; strike-aim: ahead (default) reaches walking things ahead of its front, around anything alive around it, from along its underside, and strike-lashes: N lets N be out at once); and the sprite shown on every victim
lash-shot: yes the lashes of a catastrophe are rifle shots (a straight, quick tracer line, counted by the sound) rather than tongues or tentacles; with strike-aim: around and strike-reach (the poachers' helicopter)
strike-sprite, screen-flash, storm-rain a bolt that hits the ground under the attacker, seconds of screen flashing (two quick flashes), and seconds of rain it brings (needs a weather action in the scene)
tag: gloom the scene darkens (like heavy weather) while this sprite is around
backing: 0.5 darkens the background behind the sprite's glyphs by this share (full style only), so light sprites stay readable on bright and dark backdrops
hop-period, hop-height seconds per hop and hop height in rows, for the hop behavior
destructible: no survives meteor strikes (ground sprites are destructible by default)
solid: yes the sprite may paint solid color blocks (see mask rules)
zone: forest on a mountain, where it lives: meadow, forest, woods (both), rock, snow, alpine (both) for things on the ground, sky, air (down to the rock) and low-air (over the forest and the meadow) for flyers. Ground sprites stand in a random place of the zone and never in the sky; give them habitat: ground
fall: 0.5..4 for a wind: yes particle: how fast it also falls, in rows per second (snow)
density: 1.6 for a wind: yes particle: how many fall per second, relative to sand

Mask rules: 1-9 use the creature's randomized palette, letters are fixed colors (White Red Green Blue Cyan Yellow Magenta Orange blacK Tan Nbrown Pink Light gray Sand; lower case = darker), a space is the default color. A space in the art with mask _ is opaque, with a color letter it is a solid block of that color. A color letter on a blank art cell is only allowed in sprites that declare solid: yes; the test suite rejects it everywhere else because it is almost always a misaligned mask.

Behaviors:

Behavior Effect
swim horizontal travel; leaves the world at the edge
fly travel in both axes
walk like swim, but follows the ground, dipping into craters
bob gentle vertical bobbing
rise rises and pops at the surface (bubbles)
sink falls, then lies on the ground for a while (food)
emit_bubbles now and then releases a bubble
seek_food turns towards the closest food, swims to it and eats it
hunt eats every sprite tagged prey that its mouth touches; counts kills and can feast afterwards
chase runs down the closest prey: turns towards it, changes lane to line up and closes in; gives up after its first kill
emit_dust kicks up puffs of dust behind its feet
catastrophe crosses the scene and strikes once, wiping out life or named sprites; the victims come back later, one by one
twister a tornado: weaves across the scene, whirls up sand and throws animals in its path into the air
ballistic thrown under the scene's gravity, gone when it lands (debris, dust)
meteorite streaks down and strikes the ground where its head arrives
wander drifts like swim with an organic wobble (butterflies, fireflies, birds)
hop moves in hops: rests, then jumps forward in an arc
rain a raindrop: falls to its landing row, makes a splash and is gone
ledge on a mountain: walks (or hops, with a hop-period) along a ledge of its zone, turns round where the zone or the mountain ends and now and then climbs to another ledge
soar on a mountain: flies across the scene and swoops up and down within its zone band, now and then diving (frames: wings up, wings down, gliding, diving)
leak the aquarium's leaking submarine: drives through the sea and leaves a trail of poison puffs that spread while it passes (until they fill the sea from top to bottom) and kill what they cover; the trail lingers and thins out after the submarine has gone
flood the forest's flood (an invisible sprite): a wall of water (a sparse wave at the front that fills in behind it) runs across the land from one side, tears off what it reaches, recedes and leaves mud; what was torn off is replanted late
poach the desert's helicopter: flies after the closest living thing, hovers over it and shoots at what is in reach (strike-reach, lash-shot), leaves when nothing is left or after a while
maul the mountains' yeti: climbs over the highest ridge, follows the ground down to the closest animal on the ground, kills each one it walks up to with its body (no lashing from a distance) and leaves when none is left
asteroid the moon's big meteor: falls from far away to the middle of the screen, growing through the frames of its sprite, and strikes across the whole width
clash the city's monster fight (on the lizard; the winged monster is called by it): both come in, fight, trample and flatten buildings, and the lizard walks on; the ruins are rebuilt later
avalanche the powder cloud of an avalanche: creaks on a snowy flank, then rips down it, scarring the land and burying what stands in its way
drive a city vehicle: drives along the main road in its lane, can turn off into side streets (turns) and shows its five views as it turns; stops in front of a cordon
engine the fire engine: drives to the burning building with its siren, parks, sprays water, leaves without the siren once no building burns, and cordons the site with barriers while it stays
blaze the city's rare event (an invisible sprite): sets a skyscraper on fire; the fire is either beaten or spreads and burns 3 to 5 more buildings down, which are rebuilt later

Extending: scenes

A *.scene file describes a whole world as data; sprites are shared between scenes. See assets/scenes/aquarium.scene, moon.scene and mountains.scene. 

name: aquarium
title: Aquarium
sky: #06081c #223462            # top and bottom colors
medium-colors: #1e7896 #051642  # surface and deep (omit with "medium: no")
ground-colors: #967c52 #765e3c
sky-fraction: 0.2               # share of the height above the surface (1 = all sky)
ground-rows: 2

The scene's colors are its daytime look; the night look is derived from them. The river takes at most half of the ground rows at the bottom, and a ground of more than a quarter of the height is shortened, so the scene adapts to small terminals.

Scene files are checked on load: a reference to a sprite that does not exist is reported as an error instead of being silently ignored.

Documentation

The project page links the latest release, this repository and the generated API documentation. To build the documentation yourself, install Doxygen and Graphviz and run doxygen Doxyfile (or cmake --build <dir> --target docs); the result is in docs/html.

Releasing

Work happens on develop; main only receives merges for releases. CHANGELOG.md (Keep a Changelog format) is the single source of the version and of the release text: new entries go under ## [Unreleased] as they are made, and CMake takes the version from the latest released section (## [1.2.3] 2026-11-07). A build that is not exactly that release's tagged commit reports 1.2.3-dev+<commit>. To release:

  1. On develop, rename ## [Unreleased] to ## [1.2.3] <date>, add a fresh empty ## [Unreleased] above it, and commit. scripts/release-notes.sh Unreleased previews the text before, scripts/release-notes.sh 1.2.3 after.
  2. Merge develop into main and tag it: 
    git tag 1.2.3
git push origin main 1.2.3

Pushing a tag in the form x.y.z (major.minor.fix, no v prefix) builds Diorascii on macOS, Linux and Windows, runs the tests and publishes a GitHub release with the installers and archives of every platform (see Download). The tag must match the latest released section of the changelog, or the workflow stops before building anything; that section, without its empty subsections, is the text of the release.

The release is created as a draft and only published once all three platforms built and passed their tests. The binaries report the tag as their version. Every push to main also redeploys the project page and the API documentation to GitHub Pages.

The tvOS app goes to App Store Connect (TestFlight) with one script, which generates the project with CMake, archives it, signs it with automatic signing, exports the .ipa, validates it and uploads it: 

scripts/package-tvos.sh you@example.com [TEAMID]    # DIORASCII_SKIP_UPLOAD=1 to stop after validating

If the App Store distribution profile (DIORASCII_PROFILE, default "Diorascii tvOS distribution profile", downloaded from the developer portal and installed by double-clicking it once) is installed, the app is signed manually with it and its Apple Distribution certificate, which needs no registered Apple TV; without it automatic signing is used, which does.

It uses the same keychain item for the app-specific password as the other Apple projects (AppSpecificPW, stored with xcrun altool --store-password-in-keychain-item), takes the team from the argument, DIORASCII_APPLE_TEAM or your signing certificate, and numbers the build by the commit count (DIORASCII_BUILD_NUMBER), which App Store Connect needs to grow with every upload. The app record com.ChristianAhrens.Diorascii for tvOS must exist in App Store Connect first.

The macOS asset is an installer package that places diorascii in /usr/local/bin (which every shell has in its PATH) and the license and readme in /usr/local/share/doc/diorascii. The copy CI builds is only ad-hoc signed and unsigned as a package. To build the same package with your own Developer ID signature and Apple's notarization, run on a Mac: 

scripts/package-macos.sh 1.2.3          # -> dist/Diorascii-1.2.3-macOS.pkg

Without a version it packages the project version of the changelog. The version is also what the package registers with macOS (pkgutil --pkg-info com.ChristianAhrens.Diorascii), which is how an upgrade is recognized.

It picks the first "Developer ID Application" identity in your keychain (or DIORASCII_SIGN_IDENTITY) to sign the binary with the hardened runtime, and the first "Developer ID Installer" identity (or DIORASCII_INSTALLER_IDENTITY; Apple issues it as a separate certificate) to sign the package. It submits the package with notarytool using the keychain profile APP_NOTARIZATION_CREDENTIALS_PROFILE (DIORASCII_NOTARY_PROFILE to use another, created once with xcrun notarytool store-credentials) and staples the ticket. Without identities, or with DIORASCII_ADHOC=1, it falls back to the ad-hoc signature and skips notarization, which is what the release workflow does. DIORASCII_SKIP_NOTARIZE=1 signs only. Then replace the workflow's asset on the release page with the notarized one.

The Windows installer is built by the workflow with Inno Setup from packaging/windows/diorascii.iss (installs to Program Files, adds the PATH entry, registers an uninstaller; the AppId in the script must never change, it is what lets a newer version replace an older one). The Linux .deb and .rpm come from CPack (cpack -G DEB, cpack -G RPM in the build directory; the latter needs rpmbuild) and install to /usr/bin.

Layout

src/core   headless simulation, frame buffer, menu (no I/O, deterministic per seed)
src/term   VT/ANSI encoder and the terminal backends (posix, windows)
src/audio  ambient sound playback (the synthesis itself is in src/core/ambience)
src/app    interactive session logic (shared by frontends), command line + terminal main loop
assets/    sprites and scenes, embedded at build time (cmake/embed.cmake)
pages/     landing page deployed to GitHub Pages
packaging/ installer definitions (Windows Inno Setup script)
scripts/   build and packaging helpers, release-notes.sh (changelog section -> release text),
           process-screengrabs.sh (screen recordings -> README stills and page clips)
CHANGELOG.md  per-release changes; source of the version and of the release text
tests/     dependency-free tests (ctest)
docs/ai/   notes for AI collaborators: history, architecture, conventions, rules, open findings (see CLAUDE.md)

The core only produces a grid of styled cells and consumes abstract actions, so other frontends (such as a tvOS app) can be added without touching it.

Status

Six scenes (aquarium, forest, desert, mountains, city, moon) with original sprites; feeding, predation, rain, sandstorms and tornadoes, snowstorms, meteor strikes with craters, avalanches, herds, hunting lions and bears, a day and night cycle with sleeping animals, scheduled events, rare catastrophes (kraken, thunderstorm, sandworm, avalanche, alien), synthesized ambient sound, three render styles and an ASCII menu work. A first tvOS app (see Build) shows the same scenes on the Apple TV; it is built from the same repository but not part of the release archives.
